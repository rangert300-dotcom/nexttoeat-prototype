The Story

The Story Behind Next To Eat

A New Orleans native's journey from classical music to culinary arts, fighting food deserts and building healthier futures in Central City.

Growing up in Central City, Chef Gary Netter learned early that opportunity and access aren't evenly distributed. His parents instilled in him the belief that education and excellence could be his pathway forward. At the New Orleans Center for Creative Arts (NOCCA), he pursued what came naturally—music. His classical training and undeniable talent earned him a spot performing alongside Wynton Marsalis, one of the world's greatest jazz musicians. For a young person from Central City, this was validation that his dream was possible.

But somewhere between the concert halls and the demands of a performing musician's life, Chef Gary felt a calling toward something different. He wanted to create something tangible, something that would nourish people not just culturally but physically. That instinct led him into the kitchen.

He apprenticed at New Orleans' most respected restaurants—Commander's Palace and Emeril's—training under chefs who believed that food is more than sustenance; it's a craft, a science, and an art form. Over two decades of culinary work, he mastered classical techniques, understood flavor profiles at a molecular level, and learned that the best meals honor tradition while pushing boundaries.

Chef Gary Portrait

With over 20 years of experience, Chef Gary earned recognition beyond the restaurant world. The City of New Orleans named him its official Culinary Ambassador—a title acknowledging not just his skill but his commitment to the community. But the honor came with a responsibility he couldn't ignore: while he was cooking exceptional meals for people with the means to afford them, the neighborhood where he grew up—Central City—remained a food desert. Families there lacked access to fresh, healthy food. Children didn't have the same nutritional foundation he'd been given. The disparity was personal.

Next To Eat was born from that tension. It's his answer to a question that haunted him: "What good is culinary excellence if it only reaches people who can already afford it?" He opened the Wholistic Culinary Market in Central City not as a charity, but as a real business—one that proves you can serve health-conscious, culturally grounded, medically aware meals affordably and sustainably. Y.A.M. (Young Adult Market) followed—a youth initiative rooted in culinary education, food justice, and economic opportunity.

Now, with expansion plans for Oretha Castle Haley Boulevard on the horizon, Chef Gary is proving that food as medicine, community ownership, and financial viability aren't mutually exclusive. His work is a quiet revolution—one meal, one person, one neighborhood at a time.

Looking Forward

The Vision

Next To Eat isn't just a business—it's a blueprint for what's possible when culinary excellence meets social responsibility. The Y.A.M. (Young Adult Market) initiative introduces young people from the community to careers in food, business, and nutrition, ensuring the next generation has pathways to opportunity that Chef Gary had to forge for himself.

The expansion to Oretha Castle Haley Boulevard represents a physical and symbolic growth. More meals reaching more families. A larger kitchen training space. Deeper roots in the community that raised Chef Gary and continues to deserve the best of what he can give.

Every expansion, every new meal plan, every young person trained is a step toward a future where food justice isn't aspirational—it's the norm. Where healthier living is the accessible choice. Where Central City residents have the same access to nutritionally dense, culturally affirming food as any neighborhood in the city.

That's the vision. And it starts next to where you are.
